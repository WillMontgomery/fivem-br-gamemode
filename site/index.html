Player manual

Blitz Royale

Forty-eight players. One island. One winner.
Here is everything you need to not die in the first ninety seconds.

Getting in

You start in the lobby, looking at your character. Pick a mode, hit ready, and wait for a match to form.

Solo

Everyone for themselves. Last player standing wins.

Squads

Teams of up to four. Last squad standing wins. You cannot damage your own squad — try, and nothing happens.

Playing with friends

Invite them to a party from the lobby. A party sticks around between matches — you do not have to re-form it every round. When a match starts, your party becomes a squad, topped up with solo players if you are short.

Late? If a match is still in warmup you will be dropped straight into it. If it has already taken off, you go on the next flight — you will be told which.

The drop

Everyone waits on the airstrip during warmup. This is a practice area — you can punch each other and nothing happens. Nobody takes damage on the pad.

The flight path is drawn on your map during warmup, before the plane leaves. Open the map and pick where you are going before you board; it is much harder to decide at 300 metres a second.

  1. Jump — Space when you want off the plane. Doors also open automatically near the ports and the airport.
  2. Freefall — steer with the movement keys. Look straight down to fall faster and land first.
  3. Glide — Space again to open the glider. It opens on its own if you leave it too late.
  4. Land — your gear and HUD appear the moment you touch down. Start looting immediately.

Landing first matters more than landing well. Two players in the same building is a fistfight, and the one holding a gun wins it.

Looting

You land with nothing but your fists. Everything else is on the floor or in a crate.

Picking things up

Walk up to an item and face it — it will rise off the ground and turn, and a prompt appears naming your pickup key. Tap it.

Crates need a hold instead of a tap. Holding still for a second in the open is the price of what is inside, so check your corners first.

Rarity

Everything is colour-coded, and the colour is the whole story: a rarer weapon of the same type simply hits harder.

Your inventory

Five slots, plus your fists, which you always have. Number keys 1–5 switch slots. Tab opens the full panel where you can drag to reorder, drop, and use things.

Pick up a sixth weapon with no room and whatever is in your hands drops at your feet — so it is a swap, not a refusal.

Ammo

Ammo is not in your five slots. It sits in five separate pools, shared by every gun that uses it:

Light — pistols SMG Medium — rifles Shells — shotguns Heavy — snipers & LMGs

Reloading is automatic when a magazine runs dry and you still have rounds in the pool.

Healing and shields

Health goes to 100. Shield goes to 100 on top of it, and shield is always eaten first — so a full player with full shield takes twice the punishment.

Bandages cannot finish the job. They stop at 75. To get back to full you need a Med Kit — and healing only ever comes out of crates, never off the street. If you are hurt, you have to go somewhere exposed to fix it.

Taking damage cancels whatever you are drinking or applying, and you keep the item. Heal behind cover.

Fighting

Where you hit matters

Headshots hurt — a lot — but the bonus fades with range. A headshot across the map is worth much less than the same shot from across a room. Limbs are worth less than the torso.

Range

Every weapon has a range, and damage falls off over the back half of it, down to a floor. A pistol duel at 200 metres is a waste of ammunition — close the distance or leave.

Fists

You always have them and they are a real weapon, but a poor one: about four swings to drop someone with no shield. Fine for the first ten seconds of a hot drop, not a plan.

Throwables

Grenades, sticky bombs, molotovs and smoke. Three of each, maximum — a fourth stays on the ground.

  • Grenade / Sticky — big damage in a radius. They also hurt you, so mind your feet.
  • Molotov — the fire lingers. Anyone who runs through it keeps burning, and the kill still counts as yours.
  • Smoke — no damage at all. It is the only reliable way to cross open ground or pick a teammate up under fire.

Vehicles

Drive them, hide behind them, do not fall in love with them — they are loud, and a car parked outside a building tells everyone you are in it. You cannot loot from the driver's seat.

The storm

A circle on your map marks the safe zone. Outside it you take damage every second, and it gets worse every phase. The circle shrinks in eight phases: each one holds at its current size for a while, then sweeps inward to the next.

The white line on your map is the next circle. Watch it, not the current one — by the time the wall is on you it is too late to plan.

The first circle will not kill you, so use it. Phase 1 is your loot time — half a point a second is a rounding error, and the far side of the map is often emptier and richer than the middle. By phase 5 the storm is deciding fights, and by phase 8 standing still outside it is fifteen seconds of life.

The wall is generous at its edge: if you can see the curtain and you are inside it, you are safe. You will not be chipped by a circle you are standing in.

Levels & Volts

Two things go up as you play. Levels say how long you have been around. Volts are what you spend in the market.

Both are paid at the end of a match, together, and you will see them on the verdict screen before you are back in the lobby.

What a match pays

A middling match is worth around 150. A good one is around 400.

Every match pays something. Drop badly, lose the first fight, finish last — you still get paid for turning up. Reviving a teammate pays too, because it is the least selfish thing you can do.

What does not pay

Damage dealt earns nothing, and neither does time survived. Both would pay you to shoot at people from a distance and then hide, and that is not a game anyone wants to be in.

Volts cannot be bought. There is no store, no top-up, no bundle. The only thing in this game that gives you Volts is finishing a match, and that is deliberate — see below.

The market

Everything in the market is cosmetic. Not "mostly cosmetic" — nothing you can buy changes your health, your damage, your hitbox, or how fast you move. A player who has bought everything and a player who has bought nothing arrive at the same gunfight equal.

That is the whole reason Volts can only be earned. The moment they could be bought, the sentence above would stop being true.

What is for sale

Canopies

Your parachute. Seen by everybody for the first ninety seconds of every match, which is why they are the best thing in the shop.

Smoke trails

The smoke behind you on the way down. In a squad your team colour wins, so your trail shows when you drop alone.

Weapon finishes

A colour for whatever you are holding. Gold and platinum are, if anything, easier to spot.

Buying and equipping

Open the market from the lobby. Owned items say Equip; the one you are wearing says Equipped. You always own a free default in every slot, so you can always go back.

What you buy is yours permanently. Seasons end and their items stop being for sale, but nothing you own is ever taken away or stops working.

Verdicts are not for sale. The words that land when you win are the reward for winning. You cannot buy them.

Fair play

The short version: the server decides everything that matters. Your game does not get a vote on how much damage you did, what is in your inventory, or where the loot is.

What that means for you, an honest player:

  • Nothing you do by playing normally will flag you. Shooting a teammate, punching someone in warmup, dying to your own grenade — all of that is expected, and none of it is counted against you.
  • Modded weapons and damage multipliers do not work. Not "are detected later" — they do not land. The server recalculates every hit from its own tables and ignores the number your game sent.
  • You cannot spawn items or ammo. There is no code path that adds anything to an inventory from a client message. Things enter your inventory by being picked up off the ground.
  • You cannot see where the loot is. The map layout never leaves the server. You are only ever sent the items near you.

If someone is repeatedly trying something the server did not issue them the means to do, we hear about it. Their shots were not landing anyway.

Found a bug, or someone behaving badly? Tell an admin for now — in-game reporting is being built, and this page will say so the day it lands.
